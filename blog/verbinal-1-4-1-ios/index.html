Away from your desk: Verbinal for iPhone and iPad 1.4.1

Verbinal 1.4.1 for iPhone and iPad is on the App Store, on the same page as the Mac app. It is for checking, reviewing and verifying your data away from your desk: a FITS viewer and a Cube viewer that need no account, files opened header first from Files, Mail, AirDrop or your CANFAR storage, and — once you sign in — the Portal and Compute. The Mac is still where you do the work.

Serhii Zautkin · 6 min read

It is not a port of the Mac app. There is no archive search here, no Research and no assistants. The app has four places, and they stay at the bottom of the screen: Portal, FITS, Cube and Compute. Each answers one question — is everything all right, what did it make, and is it right — and everything else waits for the Mac. It was planned in the open, in docs/plans/47-verbinal-for-iphone-and-ipad.md to 50-ios-welcome.md in the repository, and it is version 1.4.1 (18), as on the Mac.

No account needed

The viewers and your files need no account. The first time you open Verbinal, after the Terms, a welcome says what works without one — view and verify FITS, explore spectral cubes, your files on this device — and then what an account adds. Help ▸ Welcome to Verbinal shows it again. The Portal and Compute show a lock until you sign in, and the link for an account goes to canfar.net.

Two samples come with the app, under Open… ▸ Samples, so there is something to look at straight away: M31, the Andromeda Galaxy, from the Hubble Space Telescope's Wide Field/Planetary Camera in 1991; and GN-z11, a galaxy at redshift 10.6, as a NIRSpec IFU cube from JWST (program 4528, PI K. Isaak). Each carries its credit.

The FITS viewer

The image fills the screen, and a tap hides everything else. A pinch zooms about your fingers; two fingers turn the image, and it snaps to North Up within 5°; a double tap goes between Fit and 1:1 about where you tapped. Hold for the loupe: the pixels magnified eight times, the value, and the RA and Dec, with a light tick for each pixel as you move. Lift your finger and the crosshair stays, with its coordinates to copy with a tap. A compass rose takes north and east from the image's WCS, so it is right however the view is turned or flipped, and a scale bar gives a round number of arcseconds, arcminutes or degrees. On an iPad, a pointer or Apple Pencil hover reads the pixel too.

The controls are in a sheet that leaves the image usable, in four tabs:

  • Display: Auto, 99.5 % and Min–Max cuts; a histogram whose black and white points move under your finger; five stretches — Linear, Log, Sqrt, Squared and Asinh — and the colour maps, each drawn by the image's own renderer.
  • Verify: DATASUM and CHECKSUM computed from the file's own bytes, so a changed byte shows; the values — min, max, mean, σ, blanks, and how many pixels reach SATURATE when the header gives it, with the median from a sample on a big image, labelled so; and the WCS: the centre, the pixel scale, the field, the angle from north and the projection.
  • Header: the whole header, searchable.
  • HDUs: every one of them.

With two images open, hold Compare to blink between them, aligned on the sky; let go and the framing comes back. Share gives a picture of the view as it is on screen, with the crosshair and a caption line — the file, the RA and Dec, the value, the stretch and the colour map — or the file itself.

The Cube viewer

A cube opens on a slice, with a wide scrubber along the bottom and the cube's mean spectrum drawn behind it. Step through the channels with your thumb: a light tick for each channel and a firmer one on a peak, the channel's wavelength, frequency or velocity from the spectral WCS, and Play to run through them all. The slice zooms and pans as an image does. Hold a spaxel to read it and lift your finger to see its spectrum, with the channel marked on it; drag along the spectrum to move the channel.

Moment 0 sums the cube a plane at a time, finite values only, for the first question about a line: did it show up? Volume is the Mac's ray-marcher, on iPad and on iPhones with 6 GB of memory or more: one finger orbits, a pinch zooms, a tap picks the channel, and the quality steps down when the device runs warm or is in Low Power Mode. Verify gives the spectral axis — CTYPE3, CUNIT3, the first and last values, CDELT3 and RESTFRQ — the values, and the blank channels.

Files, header first

A file comes from Recent, the files you looked at on this device; from Your Storage, your CANFAR home, folders first and files newest first, FITS only unless you ask for all; from Files; or from another app, since Verbinal opens .fits, .fit, .fts and .fz from Files, Mail and AirDrop. A cube goes to Cube and an image to FITS, whichever tab you opened it from.

A file in your storage opens header first. Verbinal reads only its first blocks, up to END, and shows its HDUs, its size and the cards that say what it holds — OBJECT, the checksums — before any pixels move. You decide whether to download it, with progress and Cancel. Downloads are kept on the device up to a cap, 2 GB unless you choose another in Settings, the least recently opened going first; Keep on This Device keeps a file for good, out of the cap's reach. On This iPhone (or iPad), in the menu, shows what Verbinal holds, and Clear Downloads… clears what you no longer need.

The menu is behind your initials, in the same place on every screen: your account, your files on the device and in your storage, Settings — session reminders, how much to keep downloaded, Language and AI Compute — and Help. Language opens Verbinal's page in the Settings app, where iOS keeps each app's language. The app is in English and French, with VoiceOver.

The header, the HDUs and the checksum cards come first. You decide whether the pixels follow.

With a CANFAR account: the Portal and Compute

Sign in with your CADC account and the locks go. The Portal is a glance: the platform's load as rings; what needs your attention, first; your sessions, with Renew, events, logs and Delete; your batch jobs; and Launch, for a new session or a recent one again. A notification reminds you before a session expires. Your Storage, in the menu, browses your home with your quota: upload, download, keep, make folders and delete.

Compute runs Python or Bash on your own CANFAR session, as you — never on the device. Its runs are read from your storage, where every run is kept, so the phone shows the code and the result of runs sent from your other devices and by the Mac's assistant too, marked apart from this device's own. A run's page has its output, exit code and duration, Run Again and Copy, and the FITS files it wrote, which open header first. The code editor has smart quotes and dashes turned off: a curly quote breaks Python and Bash.

An expired session asks you to sign in again

One fix in this release reaches the Mac too, from its next release. When the app opened with an expired CADC session, it asked nothing; a renewal's state leaked into the sign-in that followed it; and nothing checked again when the app came back to the front. Now an expired session asks you to sign in again, and the app checks when it comes back to the front if the last check is 15 minutes old. It was seen on screen at launch, mid-use, with a sheet open, and with a real CADC expiry on the Mac.

Privacy, source, and how to get it

No analytics, no tracking, no third-party SDKs. Your credentials stay in the iOS Keychain; the app talks only to CADC and CANFAR, over HTTPS; session reminders are local notifications; and it does not use your location.

Verbinal for iPhone and iPad is free and open source under the Mozilla Public License 2.0, in the same repository as the Mac app, github.com/szautkin/canfar-macos, with its changelog. It runs on iOS and iPadOS 17 or newer. The viewers and your files need no account; the Portal, Compute and your storage need a free CADC account. It is on the App Store, on the same page as the Mac app.
